Water emergency in Norwalk: start here

If water is running or standing in your home, call the 24/7 emergency line at (562) 271-5850. Crews for water damage restoration Norwalk are dispatched from Santa Fe Springs, which lies to the northeast of the city, and same-day response is available when crews are free. No arrival time is promised, but you will get steps to follow while you wait.

Everything on this page applies to the 90650 ZIP code, Norwalk's only street-delivery ZIP. City Hall at 12700 Norwalk Boulevard uses it as well.

If water is still spreading

Do what is safe, and leave anything that touches wiring or sewage alone.

Find the main valve

Turn the main water valve clockwise at the house. If the valve is stuck or the leak is on the street side, call your water provider.

Check the panel first

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only when the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise call the utility or 911 before us.

Get valuables up

Lift electronics, documents and photo albums above floor level. Stack cushions and bedding on a dry bed or table.

Document the loss

Photograph the source, ceiling stains and wet floors, and note the time you found it. Keep receipts for any emergency purchases.

Norwalk water sources and why your provider matters

The city owns and runs the Norwalk Municipal Water System, which pumps groundwater from local deep wells and also buys imported Metropolitan Water District water. The city lists three well sites. Other suppliers serve parts of the community, including the City of Cerritos, Santa Fe Springs, Golden State Water Company and Liberty Utilities, so look at your bill or the city's water map.

Knowing who supplies you tells you whom to call if water is coming up at the meter or the street connection. Leaks inside the house are your responsibility, and that is where our crews work.

Sewer lines, storm drains and backups

Norwalk's Public Services Department maintains over 160 miles of sewer lines, and the city maintains 91 surface storm drains by contract. A backup that rises through a floor drain or tub is Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, meaning grossly contaminated. Stay out, keep children and pets away and call us.

For stormwater questions about street drains, the city points residents to 1-888-CLEANLA. Clear leaves from gutters and the drain in front of your property before winter rain.

Norwalk services from extraction to rebuild

Standing water starts with emergency water removal. Drying equipment is set up under water mitigation, and we monitor readings daily. After drying, water damage repair replaces drywall, baseboard and flooring that had to come out.

If a leak went unnoticed and the damage is widespread, flood damage restoration covers larger events. For wet material that has been sitting for more than 48 hours, ask about mold remediation.

What Norwalk's housing mix means for water damage

With about 27,169 housing units and a 68.3 percent owner-occupied rate, most Norwalk households own their home. Owners carry the repair decisions and insurance claim, so the first photos and a written timeline matter. The census counts 27,216 households in a city of 9.35 square miles.

If the water came from next door or upstairs in a shared building, tell the owner or manager the same day and keep your own records.

Permits and the Norwalk Building and Safety Division

The city states that building permits are required to build, enlarge, alter, repair, move, improve, remove or convert any building or structure, and for most plumbing, mechanical and electrical improvements. The division is at City Hall, 12700 Norwalk Boulevard, with permit technician appointments in Room 12. Call before a re-pipe, water heater swap or major drywall rebuild so you know what your project needs.

Flood risk and Whittier Narrows Dam in Norwalk

The city's Local Hazard Mitigation Plan ranks earthquake at 54, dam failure at 36, drought and extreme heat at 27 and flooding at 17, and it lists no FEMA repetitive-loss properties. Flooding scores medium, not low, so look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer about flood coverage, which standard homeowners policies exclude.
