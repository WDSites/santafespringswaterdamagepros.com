Dam, rivers and flood questions in Pico Rivera

Pico Rivera's General Plan says Whittier Narrows Dam effectively removed the city from the natural flood plain of the San Gabriel and Rio Hondo rivers, and that the entire city lies within the dam's flood inundation area. Everything except the river channels is FEMA Flood Zone X, and the channels are Zone A. Even Zone X properties can flood, so check the FEMA map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage.

The city says the Rio Hondo is in a lined channel, while the San Gabriel River stays natural for several miles below the dam.

If water is still spreading

These steps cover the time before your crew is dispatched.

Main valve first

Turn it clockwise at the house. If a supply line burst, open a low faucet to drain the rest.

Power only if dry

Cut breakers to wet rooms only when the panel is dry and reachable. Call the utility or 911 for any risk of shock.

Lift valuables

Move electronics, files and keepsakes above floor level. Take rugs outside only if the path is dry.

Photos before cleanup

Photograph the source, wet walls and floors, and any ruined items. Note the time you found the damage.

Water supply and sewer lines in Pico Rivera

The city's Water Division, formed as the Pico Rivera Water Authority on May 6, 1999, produces 100 percent of its supply from groundwater through 10 active wells and about 9,400 connections. Outside the city service area, the city refers residents to the Pico Water District and offers an address lookup. Check which provider serves you before calling about a meter-side problem.

The city has operated its own sewer mains and lift stations since July 1, 2014, about 110 miles in total, with Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts running regional trunk lines.

Services for Pico Rivera homes

If a pipe has burst, emergency water removal clears standing water. Water mitigation dries what remains, and water damage cleanup removes wet drywall, carpet and baseboard. Large inflows from storms are covered by flood damage restoration.

Pico Rivera housing and what to expect

The city has about 17,352 housing units, 16,866 households and a 70.5 percent owner-occupied rate. Most households own, so a loss usually means a claim the owner files. The city is compact, with parks such as Smith Park on Rosemead Boulevard and Pico Park on Beverly Boulevard. City Hall and the Sheriff station sit on Passons Boulevard.

Permits at the Pico Rivera Building Division

The Building Division is at 6615 Passons Boulevard. The city says the need for a residential permit depends on several factors, electrical and plumbing work almost always requires one, and water heater change-outs are Express Permit types. Water damage itself is not mentioned, so ask the division before rebuilding.

What the dam work means for neighbors

The Army Corps of Engineers rated Whittier Narrows Dam very high urgency in 2016, and the city expects significant construction to begin in 2026. The city expects to lose about 67 percent of its parks and open space during the work. Pico Rivera Golf Club, Streamland Park and Bicentennial Park will close indefinitely. If water issues arise near construction, report them to the city.
