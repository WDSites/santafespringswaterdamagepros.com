Three water providers, one city: what that means for a leak

Whittier is not served by a single water utility. Which one delivers to you depends on your street, so check your water bill before you call anyone about a supply-side problem.

If water is still spreading

These steps are safe to do while you wait for the crew to be dispatched.

Stop the source

Close the main valve at the house, usually near the front hose bib or beside the water heater. Turn it clockwise until the flow stops, then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines.

Power with care

Cut breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable. If water touches outlets or drips from a light fixture, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics, paperwork and shoes onto dry surfaces. Pull furniture legs off wet carpet and set foil or plastic under them.

Take photos

Photograph the source, the wet areas and damaged items before moving anything. Your insurer will ask for them, so take wide shots and close-ups.

What Uptown's 1987 earthquake history says about older Whittier buildings

The October 1, 1987 Whittier Narrows earthquake, magnitude 5.9, caused its most severe damage in the Uptown district, where unreinforced masonry buildings were concentrated. Older Uptown-area buildings deserve a careful look after a leak, because moisture can sit behind old finishes without showing.

If you own or rent an older building, ask for a moisture-meter and thermal-imaging check behind walls rather than judging by paint alone. A stain that returns after repainting usually means the wet material was never dried.
